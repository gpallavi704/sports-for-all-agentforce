GETTING STARTED
The source challenges the assumption that a physical disability automatically prevents participation in fencing. It distinguishes introductory learning from later activities requiring a fencing frame. A club can introduce equipment, weapon differences and sport vocabulary before progressing to more involved training.

ATHLETE-CENTERED COMMUNICATION
The page encourages coaches to communicate with the athlete, avoid assumptions about ability, and understand the support and facility arrangements the person needs. It emphasizes adapting the experience to the individual.

SCOPE NOTE (PROJECT EDITORIAL)
This summary deliberately excludes DIY frame construction, fastening methods, individualized coaching drills, diagnosis lists and broad assumptions about disabled athletes' preferences. It is not permission to fence without appropriate equipment or qualified supervision. The athlete and coach should agree on safe introductory arrangements. Official competition eligibility and classification require the separate formal process:
https://www.usafencing.org/parafencing-classification
